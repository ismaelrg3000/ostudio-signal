# SIGNAL Workflow & Creative Gates

[Back to the README](../README.md)

## Overview

Study → Identify → Generate → Nail art direction → Assemble and assess → Launch. The first letters spell SIGNAL.

Each stage produces an output and has a gate. Do not proceed just because a model has generated an image. The prompts for each stage are in the [prompt library](../prompts/library.md).

## S: Study the signal

Prompts: 01 (reference decoder).

### Inputs
Moodboard images, references, phrases, cultural material, objects, observations, product constraints.

### Actions
- Identify literal content.
- Research factual context where necessary.
- Separate documented facts from interpretation.
- Extract visual mechanisms.
- Identify emotional and social signals.
- Identify originality and rights risks.
- Develop possible new directions.

### Output
A reference-decoding note: the literal content, then each further statement about the reference labelled as a documented fact (with its source), an interpretation, or an invented meaning.

### Gate S
No unsupported factual claims. No direct reproduction plan. At least one useful creative principle has been identified.

## I: Identify the world

Prompts: none of its own. Prompt 01 ends with a creative brief. If you start from a theme instead of a reference, write the brief yourself from the list below.

### Actions
Define the audience hypothesis, intended emotional effect, conceptual tension, visual territory, product role, and production constraints.

### Output
A concise creative brief.

### Gate I
The brief specifies what the design should communicate and how success will be judged. A list of aesthetic adjectives alone does not pass.

## G: Generate concepts

Prompts: 02 (concept engine), 03 (social observation), 07 (visual metaphor), 10 (capsule of six to nine designs).

### Actions
Develop different ideas before generating final images. Use metaphor, contradiction, semantic inversion, visual analogy, role reversal, scale shift, cultural allusion, or another relevant mechanism.

### Output
A concept shortlist with rationale and risks.

### Gate G
The selected concept has an independent reason to exist and does not depend entirely on a protected reference or generic slogan.

## N: Nail art direction

Prompts: 04 (typography), 05 (original character), 06 (signal and noise).

### Actions
Specify subject, focal point, composition, typography, silhouette, colour, material, texture, scale, negative space, print method, and exclusions.

### Output
An art-direction specification and image-generation prompt.

### Gate N
The instructions are concrete enough to evaluate. Abstract mood words are translated into visible choices.

## A: Assemble and assess

Prompts: 08 (product adaptation), 09 (art-director critique), 15 (originality review), 16 (capsule consistency audit).

### Actions
Generate a small set of purposeful explorations. Compare them against the brief. Refine one variable at a time. Test the actual product size and print constraints.

### Output
An approved design or a clear revision request.

### Gate A
Concept, craft, originality, legibility, product fit, and technical feasibility have been reviewed. Human approval is recorded.

## L: Launch listing

Prompts: 11 (listing art direction), 12 (Etsy listing copy).

### Actions
Prepare the product, mockups, title, tags, description, sizing, care, fulfilment information, price, and contribution margin. Contribution margin is the selling price minus the costs that come with each sale, such as print cost, shipping you pay and marketplace fees.

### Output
A complete listing package ready for human review.

### Gate L
All product claims are verified; images represent the actual item accurately; required disclosures are present; the product has a viable contribution margin.

## Revision rules

- If the concept is weak, return to G.
- If the concept is good but the image fails, return to N or A.
- If the image is strong but cannot be produced reliably, revise the product specification.
- If the product is strong but the listing is unclear, revise L without changing the artwork unnecessarily.
- Do not modify multiple variables simultaneously when trying to identify a cause.

## Project record

Prompts: 13 (project record update), 14 (product expansion, for after launch).

Keep one project record, with one entry per approved design. Every other file in this repository refers to these fields.

- Design ID and title.
- Reference sources.
- Reference-decoding notes (facts, interpretations, invented meanings).
- Approved prompt, model and settings, where known.
- Visual choices: palette, type, silhouette, texture, composition.
- Revisions, critique, and rejected alternatives.
- Reason for approval, human approver, and approval date.
- Originality checks, and any rights or production risks.
- Product, supplier, variant, and production constraints.
- Final asset location (keep an untouched master).
- Listing URL, title, and tags, once published.
- Price and contribution margin calculation.
- Outstanding checks.
- Lessons learned from actual performance.
