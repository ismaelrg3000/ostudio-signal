# OStudio SIGNAL: Prompt Library

Paste your brand brief and the creative-director instruction from the [README](../README.md) above any prompt. Replace bracketed fields before running. Keep source references and final outputs together in the project record (see its fields in the [workflow](../docs/signal-workflow.md#project-record)). Each step of the workflow names the prompts that belong to it.

## Prompt 01: Reference decoder

Act as a cultural researcher and conceptual designer. Analyse the reference below without generating a derivative design.

REFERENCE: [describe the reference, or attach it if your model can read images]

Identify:
1. Literal content.
2. Verified context and unknowns.
3. Visual mechanisms: composition, typography, silhouette, colour, material, texture, scale, and contrast.
4. Emotional and social signals.
5. Conceptual mechanisms: contradiction, metaphor, wordplay, juxtaposition, ambiguity, and role reversal.
6. What makes the reference distinctive.
7. High-level principles that can be adapted.
8. Identifiable elements that must not be reproduced.

Label each statement about the reference as a documented fact (name its source), an interpretation, or an invented meaning.

Develop three distinct original directions. Explain the new subject, concept, visual treatment, and potential product. Do not assume commercial success or invent creator intent. End with a concise creative brief.

## Prompt 02: Concept engine

Develop eight original graphic concepts from [your creative brief].

For each, provide title, observation/tension, conceptual mechanism, visual expression, emotional effect, typography requirement, product, distinctive feature, originality risks, and likely failure mode.

Explore a mix of conceptual typography, original characters, visual metaphors, social observations, editorial compositions, symbols, and digital experimentation. Do not create eight variations of one idea.

Critique all eight, select the three strongest, and recommend one to develop. If none is sufficiently distinctive, explain why and restart at the concept level.

## Prompt 03: Social observation

Act as an incisive cultural observer and conceptual graphic designer.

SUBJECT: [status, hierarchy, reputation, ego, manipulation, online identity, social incentives, alienation, or another theme.]

Find a meaningful contradiction in how people behave or present themselves. Develop five treatments:
1. Restrained typographic statement.
2. Visual metaphor without words.
3. Original character or object.
4. Editorial composition.
5. Absurd or unexpected reinterpretation.

Explain the observation, visual mechanism, emotional effect, and risk of becoming obvious, preachy, or corny. Avoid generic motivational language, stock slang, predictable “alpha” imagery, and stereotypes. Describe observable behaviour and invented characters; never put psychological labels on real people.

Select the strongest concept and create precise art direction. Do not generate the final image until the concept is approved.

## Prompt 04: Typography as artwork

Create an original typographic graphic.

CONCEPT: [approved concept]
EXACT WORDING: [final copy]
PRODUCT: [product]
COMPOSITION: [stacked/cropped/asymmetric/centred/chest/back]
PALETTE: [colours]
FINISH: [clean/distressed/tactile/other intentional treatment]

Treat letterforms as the primary visual subject. Establish hierarchy through scale, spacing, alignment, line breaks, contrast, and negative space. Choose a coherent direction: monumental condensed type, editorial serif, restrained lowercase, experimental lettering, rough underground typography, or a deliberate contrast between two approaches.

Render the supplied wording exactly. Do not add extra text, fake logos, or pseudo-lettering. Keep the design suitable for the physical size. Output an isolated composition, not a mockup unless requested. Review spelling, spacing, hierarchy, balance, silhouette, and thumbnail legibility.

## Prompt 05: Original character system

Design an original character with a distinct silhouette, memorable proportions, one defining feature, and a clear but potentially ambiguous attitude.

CONCEPT: [role and emotional effect]
DESIGN MODE: [monochrome illustration/pixel art/vector/designer toy/rough print/surreal 3D]
SHAPE: [proportions, major forms, outline, facial treatment]
PALETTE: [dominant/secondary/accent]
OUTPUT: one isolated character on plain or transparent background.

Avoid generic mascot design unless required. Do not reproduce existing fictional characters, logos, costumes, or recognisable protected designs. Generate different silhouette and expression directions. Compare at sticker and garment-print size. After selection, document proportions, facial details, palette, silhouette, and permitted variations for future poses.

## Prompt 06: Signal / Noise

Create an original graphic in a signal-and-noise style: clean form meeting digital distortion.

CORE IDEA: [concept]
SUBJECT: [original object, character, or symbol]
PRODUCT: [poster/T-shirt/sticker/other]

Explore the relationship between a recognisable form and a distorted digital representation. Possible treatments include scanlines, controlled pixel displacement, chrome, iridescent materials, low-resolution fragments, photocopy grain, imperfect registration, and technical layouts. Choose only effects that strengthen the concept.

Specify focal point, crop, scale, negative space, palette, and contrast. Avoid pseudo-text, invented logos, random technical labels, and effects added purely for decoration. The graphic must function independently of a long explanation and remain recognisable at realistic product size.

## Prompt 07: Conceptual visual metaphor

Develop an original visual metaphor for [concept or social observation].

Identify the simplest literal interpretation, then find a surprising but natural visual relationship that changes how the subject is understood.

Explore scale shifts, role reversal, unexpected material, contradictory functions, familiar objects in unfamiliar contexts, and tension between appearance and behaviour.

Propose three visual mechanisms before generating. For each, describe subject, composition, dominant silhouette, emotional response, and why the metaphor works. Evaluate whether it remains understandable without a caption. Reject arbitrary symbolism and generic surrealism. Select the strongest and write a precise prompt.

## Prompt 08: Product adaptation

Prepare the approved artwork for [exact supplier product and variant].

PRODUCTION METHOD: [print/embroidery]
ARTWORK: [source file]
PRINT AREA: [verified dimensions]
AVAILABLE COLOURS: [verified options]

Preserve geometry, proportions, colour relationships, and typography. Check printable area, physical dimensions, transparency, fine lines, small text, thin gaps, contrast, colour limitations, and process suitability.

Do not distort or crop the design to make it fit. Do not add unapproved effects. Flag limitations requiring redesign. Provide a production checklist, placement recommendation, export requirements, and remaining manual checks. Do not claim production readiness before verifying the supplier specifications.

## Prompt 09: Art-director critique

Critique the supplied design as a demanding art director and production designer.

Evaluate concept, originality, composition, hierarchy, typography, silhouette, colour, texture, product suitability, actual-size legibility, fit with my brand brief, technical feasibility, and output accuracy.

Explain what works, what fails, and the single most important correction. Recommend REJECT, REVISE, or APPROVE. I make the final decision.

If revision is needed, write one targeted edit instruction preserving successful elements. If the concept itself is weak, return to concept development rather than adding decoration.

## Prompt 10: Mixed capsule

Develop a first capsule (a small launch collection) of six to nine original designs across [your creative territories from the brand brief].

For each, specify concept/title, audience, visual mechanism, product, palette/production, relationship to the collection, distinction from other designs, originality risks, and production difficulty.

Maintain a coherent sensibility while allowing different treatments. Include lead designs, supporting designs, and limited experiments. Do not create filler. Recommend the first three to finish and identify any underdeveloped territory.

## Prompt 11: Listing art direction

Create a listing-image plan for the exact product.

PRODUCT: [verified details]
ARTWORK: [approved design]
GARMENT COLOUR: [actual option]
PRINT PLACEMENT: [actual placement]
SUPPLIER: [verified supplier/product]

Plan a clear primary image, artwork close-up, placement/scale view, believable lifestyle image, size/fit image if useful, accurate colour overview, and design-story image only if valuable.

Use restrained editorial direction, believable garment texture, and controlled lighting. Do not invent material quality, product features, print texture, colours, or supplier claims. Preserve artwork proportions. Flag illustrative images that are not accurate representations.

## Prompt 12: Etsy listing copy

Act as an Etsy merchandising editor. Use only verified facts about product, supplier, material, fit, print method, sizes, colours, fulfilment, shipping, returns, and care.

Create a clear title, relevant tags, concise opening, complete description, useful design story if appropriate, accurate sizing/care/production/shipping information, AI-use disclosure where required, and a checklist of missing information.

Do not invent reviews, demand, scarcity, specifications, or commercial results. Do not use unrelated keywords or famous artists' names to attract traffic. Present for human approval; do not publish. The listing text itself carries no fact or interpretation labels.

## Prompt 13: Project record update

Update the project record with an entry for the approved design.

FIELDS: [paste the project record field list from the workflow]

Separate permanent brand rules, collection rules, and one-off decisions. Do not promote an isolated experiment into a permanent rule. Identify patterns across approved work.

## Prompt 14: Product expansion assessment

Evaluate whether an approved design or collection should extend into another product category.

Consider apparel, stickers, prints, digital design resources, templates, visual kits, and physical objects where appropriate. Assess concept fit, use case, adaptation needs, production complexity, quality control, pricing and contribution margin, customer overlap, and brand impact.

Recommend expansion only when the product makes sense independently. If considering a second shop, explain why audience, product range, and brand promise are distinct enough to justify separation.

## Prompt 15: Reference collision / originality review

Review a proposed design and its reference set.

Identify whether the concept relies too heavily on a specific existing character, logo, album cover, artwork, lyric, distinctive product, or brand identifier. Separate broad stylistic influence from potentially recognisable expression.

Ask:
- Could a viewer mistake this for official merchandise from a specific creator or franchise?
- Is the composition or wording unusually close to a specific reference?
- Does the design have an independent conceptual reason to exist?
- Would it remain interesting if the reference were removed?
- Is permission or licensing needed?

Do not give legal guarantees. Flag uncertain cases for qualified review and recommend an original redesign or licensed route.

## Prompt 16: Capsule consistency audit

Compare the selected designs as a collection.

Evaluate conceptual variety, shared point of view, visual rhythm, product balance, repeated motifs, palette relationships, and whether any design feels generic or out of place.

Do not require every piece to use the same font, colour, or rendering style. Look for shared creative judgement rather than surface uniformity.

Recommend which designs to retain, revise, remove, or develop further. Explain the rationale.
